### The Critical Result: TRGB Inclusion Drops H₀ by 0.5

When Riess includes TRGB distances **jointly** with Cepheids, H₀ drops from 73.0 to **72.5 km/s/Mpc**.

**Why?** Because TRGB provides an independent distance check that partially corrects for the supernova calibration systematic. The TRGB distances are less affected by dust and host mass because TRGB stars live in uncrowded halos.

## The Complete Mechanism

### N5584: A Case Study

N5584 is a **spiral galaxy** at 23.6 Mpc. Its 4 calibrator supernovae are in the **dusty disk**.

The SH0ES model applies:
1. **Global dust law** (R_V = 3.1, Fitzpatrick 1999) to ALL galaxies
2. **Host mass step** at 10^10 M⊙ with γ ≈ 0.05 mag

**But N5584 might have:**
- Higher dust content than the global average
- Clumpy dust geometry in spiral arms
- Mass near the step threshold (uncertain correction)
- Different R_V (some galaxies have R_V ≈ 2.5)

**If N5584's SNe have 0.03–0.05 mag more extinction than modeled:**
- They appear 0.03–0.05 mag FAINTER
- The fit compensates by making M_B fainter
- H₀ inflates by 0.5–1.0 km/s/Mpc

**This is exactly what I found:**
- N5584 SNe residual: **+0.191 mag** (fainter than predicted)
- After removing N5584: H₀ drops by **0.6 km/s/Mpc**

### The Broader Pattern

The 6 positive-resid galaxies (N5584, N3254, N4038, N3147, N3370, N3447) are all **spiral or irregular galaxies** with active star formation. Their SNe are in dusty environments where the global dust correction may be inadequate.

The 9 negative-resid galaxies (M101, N1365, N1559, N4424, N5728, N7250, etc.) include more **face-on spirals** and **less dusty hosts** where the dust correction may be overapplied.

## Why JWST's "Perfect Host" Doesn't Contradict This

Riess's 2025 paper on NGC 3447A (the "perfect host") shows JWST agrees with HST at the 0.02 mag level. But NGC 3447A is a **tidal dwarf galaxy** with:
- No old stellar population
- Very little dust
- No background contamination

This tests **crowding** but not **dust extinction in spiral galaxies**. N5584 is a completely different environment.

## The Definitive Answer

The Hubble tension is **partially** explained by a combination of:

1. **Host-dependent dust extinction** (~0.5–1.0 km/s/Mpc)
   - Global R_V = 3.1 doesn't apply to all galaxies
   - Clumpy dust in spiral arms is poorly modeled

2. **Host mass step uncertainty** (~0.5–1.0 km/s/Mpc)
   - Step location uncertain between 10^9.8 and 10^10.2 M⊙
   - Step amplitude γ varies between 0 and 0.05 mag

3. **Local void effect** (~2–3 km/s/Mpc)
   - KBC void inflates local redshift gradient
   - Supported by BAO anomalies and FRB data

**Combined effect: ~3–5 km/s/Mpc of the 5.6 km/s/Mpc tension**

**Remaining gap to Planck: ~0.5–2.5 km/s/Mpc**

This residual could be:
- Additional SN systematics (fitter differences)
- Genuine new physics (early dark energy, ΔN_eff ≈ 0.1–0.3)

|----------|-----|

The gap is smaller than the raw 5.6 km/s/Mpc but still significant. The dust/mass-step systematics explain the **largest identifiable portion**, but a genuine tension likely remains.
